## Studied network devices.

### PCs / End Devices:
- The sender is the device that sends the data message. It can be a computer, workstation, telephone handset, video camera, and so on. The receiver is the device that receives the message. It can be a computer, workstation, telephone handset, television, and so on. The duty of the application, transport, and network layers is end-to-end, while the duty of the data-link and physical layers is hop-to-hop.
  
### Switches:
- Switches are devices capable of creating temporary connections between two or more devices linked to the switch. A link-layer switch (or switch) operates in both the physical and the data-link layers. As a link-layer device, a switch can check the MAC addresses. A link-layer switch has filtering capability. It can check the destination address of a frame and can decide from which outgoing port the frame should be sent. A transparent switch is a switch in which the stations are completely unaware of the switch's existence. If a switch is added or deleted from the system, reconfiguration of the stations is unnecessary. It maintains a dynamic table that maps addresses to ports (interfaces) automatically. Advantages of Switches: Collision Elimination and Connecting Heterogeneous Devices.
  
### Routers:
- A router is a three-layer device; it operates in the physical, data-link, and network layers. As a physical-layer device, it regenerates the signal it receives. As a link-layer device, the router checks the physical addresses (source and destination) contained in the packet. As a network-layer device, a router checks the network-layer addresses. A router is an internetworking device; it connects independent networks to form an internetwork. A router has a physical and logical (IP) address for each of its interfaces. A router changes the link-layer address of the packet when it forwards the packet.
  
## Studied cable types.
- Guided media provide a physical conduit from one device to another. The study covers the three main types:

### Twisted-Pair Cables:
- A twisted pair consists of two conductors (normally copper), each with its own plastic insulation, twisted together. One of the wires is used to carry signals to the receiver, and the other is used only as a ground reference. The most common twisted-pair cable used in communications is referred to as unshielded twisted-pair (UTP). Shielded twisted-pair (STP) cable has a metal foil or braided mesh covering that encases each pair of insulated conductors; it is bulkier and more expensive. Twisted-pair cables are used in telephone lines to provide voice and data channels.

### Coaxial Cable:
- Coaxial cable (or coax) carries signals of higher frequency ranges than those in twisted-pair cable. Coax has a central core conductor enclosed in an insulating sheath, encased in an outer conductor of metal foil. The outer metallic wrapping serves both as a shield against noise and as the second conductor. The whole cable is protected by a plastic cover. Coaxial cables are categorized by their Radio Government (RG) ratings: RG-59 (Cable TV), RG-58 (Thin Ethernet), and RG-11 (Thick Ethernet). Coaxial cable has a much higher bandwidth. It was widely used in analog telephone networks and later in digital telephone networks.

### Fiber-Optic Cable:
- A fiber-optic cable is made of glass or plastic and transmits signals in the form of light. Optical fibers use reflection to guide light through a channel. Current technology supports two modes: multimode (step-index or graded-index) and single mode.
- Advantages: Higher bandwidth (supports dramatically higher data rates than twisted-pair or coaxial), Less signal attenuation (signal can run for 50 km without regeneration), Immunity to electromagnetic interference, Resistance to corrosive materials, Light weight, and Greater immunity to tapping.
- Disadvantages: Complex installation and maintenance requiring expertise, Unidirectional light propagation, and High cost of cable and interfaces.

## Notes for the Team 
### PCs / End Devices: (Ref: Ch1 p.5 - Ch2 p.37)
- Sender sends the data message.
- Receiver receives the data message.
- Communication at the Application, Transport, and Network layers is end-to-end.
- Data-Link and Physical layers work hop-to-hop.

### Switches: (Ref: Ch17 p.495-500)
- Operates at the Physical and Data-Link layers.
- Uses MAC addresses.
- Forwards frames only to the correct destination port.
- Uses a MAC table.
- Advantages: Collision elimination and connecting heterogeneous devices.

### Routers: (Ref: Ch17 p.501)
- Operates at the Physical, Data-Link, and Network layers.
- Uses IP addresses for routing.
- Connects different networks.
- Has an IP address for each interface.
- Changes the link-layer address when forwarding packets.

### Cable Types (Guided Media): (Ref: Ch7 p.187-197)
- Twisted Pair: Most common cable (UTP/STP); used for voice and data.
- Coaxial: Higher bandwidth than twisted pair; used in cable TV and older Ethernet.
- Fiber Optic: Uses light transmission; high bandwidth, long distance, immune to EMI.
